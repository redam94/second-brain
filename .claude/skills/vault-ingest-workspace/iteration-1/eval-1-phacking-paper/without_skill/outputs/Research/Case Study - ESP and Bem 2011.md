# Case Study: ESP and Bem (2011)

From [[Gelman and Loken 2013 - The Garden of Forking Paths]], Section 2.2.

## Original Study

Bem (2011) claimed evidence for extrasensory perception (precognition) in college students, published in the *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*. The paper generated enormous attention and controversy.

## Multiplicity Problems

### Nine Experiments, Many Outcomes
- The paper included nine different experiments with many statistically significant results
- Each experiment had multiple degrees of freedom allowing the researcher to find significance

### One-to-Many Mapping
- A single *scientific* hypothesis (precognitive ability) maps to multiple *statistical* hypotheses
- Significant results for erotic images but not non-erotic images were reported
- Had the pattern been reversed, or appeared only for men, or only in the second half of trials, each outcome could have been framed as supporting the theory

### Bem's Defense and Its Limitations
- Bem insisted his hypotheses were not exploratory and were derived from prior "presentiment" research
- Gelman and Loken's response: even if the scientific hypothesis was pre-specified, the statistical tests were not -- a data-dependent analysis need not *look* post hoc

## Connection to Dead Salmon MRI

The example parallels Bennett et al. (2009), who found statistically significant patterns in an MRI of a dead Atlantic salmon, illustrating how generally-accepted research practices can find significance anywhere.

## Lesson

The distinction between a pre-specified scientific hypothesis and the statistical tests used to evaluate it is crucial. One scientific hypothesis can generate many statistical tests, creating hidden [[Multiple Comparisons Problem|multiplicity]].
