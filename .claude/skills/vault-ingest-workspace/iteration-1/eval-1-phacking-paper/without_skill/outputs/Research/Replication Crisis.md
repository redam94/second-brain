# Replication Crisis

The ongoing recognition that many published scientific findings, particularly in psychology and social sciences, fail to replicate when independent researchers attempt to reproduce them.

## Connection to the Garden of Forking Paths

[[Gelman and Loken 2013 - The Garden of Forking Paths]] provides a theoretical explanation for why so many findings fail to replicate: [[Researcher Degrees of Freedom]] create a [[Multiple Comparisons Problem]] that inflates published effect sizes and produces false positives at rates far exceeding the nominal 5% level.

The paper emphasizes that this does not require researchers to be dishonest or to engage in deliberate [[P-Hacking]]. The problem arises from the structure of the research process itself, particularly in fields characterized by small samples, noisy measurements, and small effects.

## Key Contributing Factors

- [[Researcher Degrees of Freedom]] in data analysis
- Low statistical power (Button et al., 2013)
- Publication bias favoring significant results
- The "file drawer problem" suppressing null results
- Lack of [[Pre-registration]]
