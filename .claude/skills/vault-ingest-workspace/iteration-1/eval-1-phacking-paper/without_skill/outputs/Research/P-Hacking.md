# P-Hacking

The practice of trying out multiple analyses on a dataset and selectively reporting those that produce statistically significant results (p < .05).

## Terminology

Also called "fishing," "data dredging," or "fishing expeditions." The term was popularized by [[Simmons Nelson and Simonsohn 2011]].

## Gelman and Loken's Critique of the Term

In [[Gelman and Loken 2013 - The Garden of Forking Paths]], the authors argue that the terms "p-hacking" and "fishing" are misleading because they imply researchers are consciously trying many analyses. The real problem is broader: researchers can unknowingly exploit [[Researcher Degrees of Freedom]] by making data-contingent analysis choices that feel like the only reasonable option. The authors advocate thinking in terms of the [[Garden of Forking Paths]] rather than deliberate fishing.

## Distinction from Researcher Degrees of Freedom

P-hacking is a subset of the broader problem of [[Researcher Degrees of Freedom]]:
- **P-hacking** implies deliberate, conscious exploration of multiple tests
- **Researcher degrees of freedom** encompasses unconscious, theory-driven but data-contingent choices
