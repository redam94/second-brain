# Garden of Forking Paths

A metaphor introduced by [[Andrew Gelman]] and [[Eric Loken]] (drawing on Borges's 1941 short story) to describe the branching tree of possible data analyses that a researcher could reasonably pursue given different observed data.

## Definition

The "garden of forking paths" refers to the full space of data-analysis decisions a researcher faces -- choice of variables, exclusion criteria, subgroup analyses, interaction terms, outcome measures, and coding rules. Each decision point is a fork. The researcher walks one path through the garden, but the statistical properties of their result depend on the entire garden, not just the path taken.

## Key Insight

Even when a researcher performs only one analysis, the fact that they *would have* performed a different analysis under different data means the result is subject to a [[Multiple Comparisons Problem]]. The analysis is data-contingent even if no explicit comparison shopping occurs.

## Distinction from P-Hacking

Unlike [[P-Hacking]], the garden of forking paths does not require conscious exploration of multiple analyses. Researchers may genuinely believe their analysis was the only reasonable one -- but that belief is conditional on the data they observed.
