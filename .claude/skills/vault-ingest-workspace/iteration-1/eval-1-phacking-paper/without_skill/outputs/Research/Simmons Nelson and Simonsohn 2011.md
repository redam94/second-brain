# Simmons, Nelson, and Simonsohn (2011)

**Full title:** "False-positive psychology: Undisclosed flexibility in data collection and analysis allow presenting anything as significant."

**Published in:** *Psychological Science* 22, 1359--1366.

## Significance

This paper introduced the concept of [[Researcher Degrees of Freedom]] (also called "p-hacking") and demonstrated through simulation how undisclosed flexibility in data collection and analysis can produce false-positive rates far exceeding 5%.

## Relationship to Garden of Forking Paths

[[Gelman and Loken 2013 - The Garden of Forking Paths]] extends this work by arguing that the problem does not require conscious "flexibility" or deliberate exploration. Even researchers who perform a single analysis can be subject to the same problem if their analysis choices are data-contingent.
