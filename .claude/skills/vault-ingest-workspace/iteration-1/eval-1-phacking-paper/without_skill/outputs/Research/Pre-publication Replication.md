# Pre-publication Replication

A research strategy in which an initial exploratory study is followed by a confirmatory replication with a pre-registered protocol, both conducted before publication.

## Proposal

Discussed in [[Gelman and Loken 2013 - The Garden of Forking Paths]], Section 4.3, as a practical alternative to full [[Pre-registration]] for addressing [[Researcher Degrees of Freedom]].

## Example: Nosek, Spies, and Motyl (2013)

Nosek et al. found a large, statistically significant relationship between perceptual judgment and political attitudes. Rather than simply publishing, they:
1. Gathered a large new sample
2. Performed a replication with predetermined protocols and data analysis
3. The replication was unsuccessful (p = .59) despite having over 99% power based on the original estimates

This example illustrates both the value of pre-publication replication and the fragility of initial statistically significant findings.

## Advantages

- Preserves exploratory freedom in the first study
- Provides genuine confirmatory evidence in the second
- Feasible for inexpensive, noninvasive studies (common in psychology)
