Abstract (from paper):
  This paper identifies a set of assumptions sufficient to ensure that a greedy search
  algorithm in the space of equivalence classes of Bayesian networks recovers the
  equivalence class of the generative distribution in the large-sample limit. Given
  these assumptions, the paper presents a greedy search algorithm—called Greedy
  Equivalence Search (GES)—and proves that the algorithm is correct. GES is motivated
  by a result due to Meek (1997), called the "Meek conjecture," which asserts that any
  two distinct Bayesian-network structures that encode the same set of independence
  assertions can always be connected by a sequence of single-edge insertions and
  deletions such that each Bayesian-network structure in the sequence is an independence
  map of the other. This paper proves the Meek conjecture, and uses the result to
  guarantee the correctness of GES.

Key contents:
  - Proof of the Meek Conjecture (Theorem 15)
  - GES algorithm: Forward Equivalence Search (FES) + Backward Equivalence Search (BES)
  - Turning operators (Insert, Delete) for moving between equivalence classes
  - Consistency guarantee for GES under faithfulness assumption
  - Relationship to Chickering (2002a) "Learning Equivalence Classes of Bayesian-Network
    Structures"
