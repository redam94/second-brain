Title: Optimal Structure Identification With Greedy Search
Author: David Maxwell Chickering
Year: 2002
Venue: Journal of Machine Learning Research, Vol. 3, pp. 507-554
arXiv / DOI: https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v3/chickering02b.html
Also at: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=342392

Abstract (from web search):
Proves the "Meek Conjecture": if a DAG H is an independence map of a DAG G then there
exists a finite sequence of edge additions and covered edge reversals in G that transforms
G into H such that every intermediate graph is also an independence map of H. Using this
result, the paper shows that the two-phase greedy search algorithm (GES) over equivalence
classes of Bayesian network structures provably identifies a perfect map of the generative
distribution if one exists, in the large-sample limit. GES uses insert and delete operators
defined on equivalence classes (CPDAGs), and is exact in the identifiable case.

Key contributions:
1. Proof of the Meek conjecture (covered edge reversals can reach any equivalent DAG)
2. The GES (Greedy Equivalence Search) algorithm — two phases:
   - Forward phase: greedily adds edges (insert operator on CPDAG)
   - Backward phase: greedily removes edges (delete operator on CPDAG)
3. Consistency result: under faithfulness + causal sufficiency, GES recovers true CPDAG in large-sample limit
4. Efficient CPDAG representation of the search space
