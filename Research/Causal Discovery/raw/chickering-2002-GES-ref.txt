Title: Optimal Structure Identification With Greedy Search
Authors: David Maxwell Chickering
Journal: Journal of Machine Learning Research, Vol. 3, pp. 507-554, November 2002
JMLR page: https://jmlr.org/papers/v3/chickering02b.html
PDF: https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume3/chickering02b/chickering02b.pdf (JMLR open access)

Relevance: Introduces and proves the correctness of GES (Greedy Equivalence Search), a two-phase
score-based algorithm for causal DAG structure learning. Key results:
1. Proof of the Meek conjecture (that the score is always improvable if the current equivalence class
   is not the true one, under faithfulness and consistent scoring).
2. Algorithm correctness: GES recovers the Markov equivalence class of the true DAG in the
   large-sample limit under CMC + faithfulness + decomposable, consistent scoring.
3. Forward phase (FES) operators and backward phase (BES) operators with proofs of validity.

Also relevant: Meek (1995) — "Causal inference and causal explanation with background knowledge"
for the Meek rules (R1-R4) for CPDAG orientation.
Ramsey et al. (2017) — "A million variables and more: the Fast Greedy Equivalence Search (FGES)
algorithm for optimizing Bayesian networks and Gaussian graphical models" (International Journal of
Data Science and Analytics 3, pp. 219-230) for the parallelized FGES variant.
