Title: Optimal Structure Identification with Greedy Search
Author: David Maxwell Chickering
Journal: Journal of Machine Learning Research
Volume: 3, Pages: 507–554, November 2002
URL: https://jmlr.org/papers/v3/chickering02b.html

Abstract:
This paper proves the so-called Meek Conjecture, which concerns transforming one DAG into
another within the same Markov equivalence class through a sequence of edge additions and
covered edge reversals. The paper also describes a two-phase greedy search algorithm (GES —
Greedy Equivalence Search) that operates directly in the space of Markov equivalence classes
(represented as CPDAGs). Phase 1 (forward equivalence search, FES) greedily adds edges to
improve a score; Phase 2 (backward equivalence search, BES) greedily removes edges. Under
the Faithfulness assumption and with a consistent scoring function (e.g. BIC), GES is shown
to be consistent. Experiments on synthetic and real datasets demonstrate the two-phase approach
works well with finite samples, matching or outperforming earlier structure-learning algorithms.

Key contributions:
1. Proof of the Meek Conjecture (transformational characterization of equivalence class
   traversal — enables safe edge operations in CPDAG space)
2. The GES algorithm: two-phase (FES + BES) greedy search on equivalence classes
3. Characterization of Insert and Delete operators in CPDAG space
4. Consistency proof under faithfulness + consistent scoring
5. Empirical evaluation showing competitive performance
