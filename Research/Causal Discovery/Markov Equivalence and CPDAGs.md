# Markov Equivalence and CPDAGs

> [!summary]
> Structure learning from observational data cannot identify a unique DAG — only a **Markov
> equivalence class** (MEC) of DAGs that encode the same conditional independence structure.
> The MEC is compactly represented as a **CPDAG** (Completed Partially Directed Acyclic Graph),
> also called the *essential graph*, in which some edges are directed (and shared by all members)
> and others are undirected (direction varies within the MEC). Algorithms like [[PC Algorithm]]
> and [[Greedy Equivalence Search]] return the CPDAG, not a single DAG.

## Overview

When we observe data generated by a DAG $G^*$ with distribution $P^*$, and we want to recover
$G^*$ from $P^*$, we face a fundamental limit: **many different DAGs can encode exactly the
same set of conditional independence (CI) relationships**. Two such DAGs are called
*Markov equivalent*. No observational data alone can distinguish them.

This is not merely a practical limitation — it is a mathematical impossibility. Two Markov
equivalent DAGs generate the same marginal and conditional distributions over all observed
variables, so no test, Bayesian or frequentist, can differentiate them from observational
data alone (without interventions).

The *best we can do* from purely observational data is to identify the **Markov equivalence
class** — the set of all DAGs consistent with the observed CI structure. This class is
canonically represented by the **CPDAG**.

## Main Content

### Markov Equivalence

> [!definition] Definition: Markov Equivalence (Frydenberg 1990; Verma & Pearl 1990)
> Two DAGs $G_1$ and $G_2$ on the same vertex set $\mathbf{V}$ are **Markov equivalent** if
> they encode the same set of conditional independence relations — that is, if every CI statement
> that follows from $G_1$ via d-separation also follows from $G_2$, and vice versa.
>
> **Graphical characterisation (Verma & Pearl 1990):** $G_1 \equiv G_2$ if and only if they
> have the same:
> 1. **Skeleton** (same edges, ignoring direction), AND
> 2. **Unshielded colliders** (v-structures) — triples $X \to Z \leftarrow Y$ where $X$ and $Y$
>    are not adjacent, called *unshielded* or *unfaithful* colliders.
>
> Two shielded colliders ($X \to Z \leftarrow Y$ with $X$ and $Y$ adjacent) can be flipped
> to $X \leftarrow Z \to Y$ or $X \to Z \to Y$ while staying in the same MEC.

^def-markov-equiv

> [!example] Example: A three-node MEC
> Consider three nodes $X, Y, Z$. The DAGs $X \to Y \to Z$ and $X \leftarrow Y \to Z$ and
> $X \to Y \leftarrow Z$ (where $X-Z$ not adjacent) form different MECs:
>
> - MEC 1: $\{X \to Y \to Z,\; X \leftarrow Y \to Z,\; X \leftarrow Y \leftarrow Z,\; X \to Y \leftarrow Z^*\}$
>   Wait — $X \to Y \leftarrow Z$ is actually a different MEC because it contains an unshielded
>   collider at $Y$.
>
> More carefully:
> - **MEC A:** $\{X - Y - Z\}$ all three DAGs without v-structures:
>   $X \to Y \to Z$, $X \leftarrow Y \to Z$, $X \leftarrow Y \leftarrow Z$. CPDAG: $X - Y - Z$.
> - **MEC B:** Just $X \to Y \leftarrow Z$ (unshielded collider at $Y$; unique MEC member
>   since any reorientation creates a different v-structure or a cycle). CPDAG: $X \to Y \leftarrow Z$.

### CPDAG (Essential Graph)

> [!definition] Definition: CPDAG (Andersson, Madigan & Perlman 1997)
> The **CPDAG** (Completed Partially Directed Acyclic Graph) of a Markov equivalence class
> $[\![G]\!]$ is the unique mixed graph $\mathcal{E}$ over $\mathbf{V}$ such that:
>
> 1. **Skeleton:** $\mathcal{E}$ has the same skeleton as every member of $[\![G]\!]$.
> 2. **Directed edges:** An edge $X \to Y$ appears in $\mathcal{E}$ if and only if $X \to Y$
>    appears in *every* DAG in $[\![G]\!]$ (i.e., it is *compelled*).
> 3. **Undirected edges:** An edge $X - Y$ appears in $\mathcal{E}$ if and only if the edge
>    appears in both orientations in different members of $[\![G]\!]$ (i.e., it is *reversible*).
>
> The CPDAG is also called the **essential graph** of $G$.
>
> **Compelled vs. reversible edges (Chickering 1995):** An edge $X \to Y$ in $G$ is
> *compelled* if every member of $[\![G]\!]$ contains $X \to Y$; otherwise it is *reversible*.
> An edge is reversible if and only if it is a *covered edge* — meaning $\text{pa}(X) = \text{pa}(Y) \setminus \{X\}$.

^def-cpdag

### Meek's Orientation Rules

After skeleton discovery and v-structure orientation (see [[PC Algorithm]]), a set of orientation
rules — Meek's rules — propagates implied orientations to all remaining undirected edges without
creating new v-structures or directed cycles.

> [!theorem] Meek's Orientation Rules (Meek 1995) — Rules R1–R4
> Given a partially oriented graph with the correct skeleton and v-structures, apply the
> following rules iteratively until no more edges can be oriented:
>
> **R1** (avoid new v-structures): If $Z - X \to Y$ and $Z \not\sim Y$, orient $Z \to X$.
> *(Orienting $Z \leftarrow X$ would require $Z$ to be a non-collider, but $Z \not\sim Y$ means
> this would create a new v-structure $Z \leftarrow X \to Y$ with $Z \not\sim Y$, impossible.)*
>
> **R2** (avoid directed cycle): If $X \to Z \to Y$ and $X - Y$, orient $X \to Y$.
> *(Orienting $X \leftarrow Y$ would create a directed cycle $X \to Z \to Y \leftarrow X$.)*
>
> **R3** (Zhangs's rule): If $X - Z_1 \to Y$, $X - Z_2 \to Y$, $Z_1 \not\sim Z_2$, and $X - Y$,
> orient $X \to Y$.
>
> **R4**: If $X - Z \to W \to Y$ and $X - Y$ and $X \not\sim W$, orient $X \to Y$.
>
> **Completeness (Meek 1995):** These four rules are *complete*: every edge in the CPDAG that
> can be oriented from the skeleton + v-structures alone is oriented by some sequence of R1–R4
> applications.

^thm-meek-rules

### The Meek Conjecture and Covered Edges

A key result for the correctness of GES is the **Meek Conjecture**, proven by Chickering (2002):

> [!theorem] Meek Conjecture (Chickering 2002, Theorem 15)
> Let $G$ and $H$ be two DAGs in the same Markov equivalence class. Then there exists a
> sequence of DAGs $G = G_0, G_1, \ldots, G_k = H$ such that each $G_{i+1}$ is obtained from
> $G_i$ by a single **covered edge reversal**.
>
> A **covered edge** $X \to Y$ in $G$ is one where
> $$\text{pa}_G(X) = \text{pa}_G(Y) \setminus \{X\}.$$
> Reversing a covered edge preserves the MEC: $G_i$ and $G_{i+1}$ are always Markov equivalent.
>
> **Significance for GES:** This theorem shows that the MEC space is *connected* under covered
> edge reversals, which means a greedy search can navigate the MEC space without ever leaving
> valid MECs. This justifies the GES operator design — see [[Greedy Equivalence Search]].

^thm-meek-conjecture

### Identifiability Limitations

The CPDAG identifies some edge directions but not all:
- **Compelled edges** (directed in the CPDAG) are identifiable from observational data alone.
- **Reversible edges** (undirected in the CPDAG) cannot be oriented without additional
  assumptions or interventional data.

To achieve full DAG identification from observational data, additional assumptions are required:
- **Linear non-Gaussian additive models (LiNGAM):** If the noise is non-Gaussian, the full DAG
  is identifiable (Shimizu et al. 2006).
- **Additive noise models (ANMs):** With nonlinear structural equations and additive noise, full
  identifiability holds (Hoyer et al. 2009, Peters et al. 2014).
- **Interventional data:** Direct interventions can orient reversible edges.

## Connections

- **d-separation** in [[Directed Acyclic Graphs]]: the graphical criterion that defines what CI
  relations a DAG implies. Two DAGs are Markov equivalent iff they have the same d-separation
  relations.
- **PC Algorithm** ([[PC Algorithm]]): returns the CPDAG by discovering the skeleton then
  orienting v-structures and applying Meek's rules.
- **GES** ([[Greedy Equivalence Search]]): searches over CPDAGs directly using the MEC space.
- **NOTEARS** ([[NOTEARS - Overview]]): returns a DAG (not CPDAG) via continuous optimization;
  unlike PC/GES, it identifies a specific member of the MEC rather than the full class.
- **DAG Structure Learning Problem** ([[DAG Structure Learning Problem]]): the score-based
  formulation that both GES and NOTEARS build on.
