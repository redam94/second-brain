# Gauge Theory

> [!summary]
> Gauge theories are field theories whose Lagrangians are invariant under local symmetry transformations (Lie group actions). They underpin the Standard Model of particle physics and general relativity. This note is a minimal stub; the vault's focus is statistics and econometrics.

> [!warning] Out of Scope

## Overview

A gauge theory requires that the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}$ be invariant under local transformations of a symmetry (gauge) group $G$. This invariance necessitates the introduction of gauge fields (one per group generator); when quantized, these fields give rise to gauge bosons. Examples: QED ($G = \text{U}(1)$, photon), electroweak theory ($G = \text{U}(1) \times \text{SU}(2)$), QCD ($G = \text{SU}(3)$, gluons).
