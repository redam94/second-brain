# Vine Copulas — Source References

## Primary sources

### Aas, Czado, Frigessi & Bakken (2009)
"Pair-copula constructions of multiple dependence"
*Insurance: Mathematics and Economics*, 44(2), 182–198. DOI: 10.1016/j.insmatheco.2007.02.001

Working-paper preprint (SFB 386, Paper 487, 2006):
https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/1855/1/paper_487.pdf

**Key content:** First inferential treatment of PCCs: density factorisation, C-vine and D-vine
definitions, h-function recursion, sequential ML estimation, 4-variable financial application.

---

### Brechmann & Schepsmeier (2013)
"Modeling Dependence with C- and D-Vine Copulas: The R Package CDVine"
*Journal of Statistical Software*, 52(3), 1–27. DOI: 10.18637/jss.v052.i03

Official open-access PDF:
https://www.jstatsoft.org/index.php/jss/article/view/v052i03/v52i03.pdf

**Key content:** Complete sequential and joint MLE algorithms, pair-copula family selection (AIC/BIC,
Vuong test), rotation conventions, software API documentation.

---

### Czado & Nagler (2022)
"Vine Copula Based Modeling"
*Annual Review of Statistics and Its Application*, 9, 453–477. DOI: 10.1146/annurev-statistics-040220-101153

TU Delft repository:
https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:6487cd04-d375-461e-a3a7-4464ffc60d80

**Key content:** Modern review covering R-vine structure selection (Dißmann algorithm), simplified vine
assumption testing, truncated vines, and comparison of vine vs. factor vs. elliptical copulas.

---

### Bedford & Cooke (2002)
"Vines — a new graphical model for dependent random variables"
*Annals of Statistics*, 30(4), 1031–1068.

**Key content:** Formal definition of regular vines as nested tree sequences with the proximity
condition; proves any multivariate density has a valid vine decomposition.

---

### Joe (1996)
"Families of m-variate distributions with given margins and m(m-1)/2 bivariate dependence parameters"
In *Distributions with Fixed Marginals and Related Topics* (IMS Lecture Notes vol. 28), pp. 120–141.

**Key content:** First construction of pair-copula models for m-variate distributions; introduced
what later authors call D-vine and C-vine structures.
