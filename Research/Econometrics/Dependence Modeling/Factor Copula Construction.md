# Factor Copula Construction

> [!summary]
> A factor copula is the copula of a latent vector $\mathbf{X}$ generated by a simple linear factor model: each $X_i$ equals a (loaded) common factor $Z$ plus an idiosyncratic shock $\varepsilon_i$. The copula of $\mathbf{X}$ is used as the model for the copula of the observable data $\mathbf{Y}$, while $\mathbf{X}$'s implied marginals are discarded. The class generally has **no closed-form density**; only the all-Gaussian case yields a known (Gaussian, equicorrelation) copula.

## Overview

The construction separates dependence from marginals (per Sklar). One specifies a tractable latent factor model, simulates from it to recover its copula $\mathbf{C}(\boldsymbol{\theta})$, and pairs that copula with separately-estimated marginals $F_i$. The richness of the dependence — symmetry, tail behaviour, heterogeneity — is controlled entirely by the choice of factor and idiosyncratic distributions and the loadings. This note states the equidependence and flexible-weight constructions; multi-factor extensions are in [[Multi-Factor and Block Dependence Structures]].

## Main Content

> [!definition] Simple (equidependence) factor copula
> Based on $N+1$ latent variables, for $i = 1, 2, \dots, N$:
> $$ X_i = Z + \varepsilon_i $$
> $$ Z \sim F_z(\boldsymbol{\theta}), \quad \varepsilon_i \sim \text{iid } F_\varepsilon(\boldsymbol{\theta}), \quad Z \perp\!\!\!\perp \varepsilon_i \;\forall i $$
> $$ [X_1, \dots, X_N]' \equiv \mathbf{X} \sim \mathbf{F}_x = \mathbf{C}(G_1(\boldsymbol{\theta}), \dots, G_N(\boldsymbol{\theta}); \boldsymbol{\theta}) $$
> where $Z$ is the **common factor**, $\varepsilon_i$ the **idiosyncratic shocks**, $G_i$ the marginals of the latent $X_i$, and $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ the copula parameters. The copula $\mathbf{C}(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ of $\mathbf{X}$ is the model for the copula of $\mathbf{Y}$. Crucially the latent marginals $G_i$ are **discarded**: $F_i \neq G_i$ in general; only the copula of $\mathbf{X}$ is retained, and observable marginals $F_i$ are specified/estimated separately.
^def-simple

> [!definition] Closed-form case and equidependence
> If $F_z$ and $F_\varepsilon$ are **both Gaussian**, then $\mathbf{X}$ is multivariate Gaussian, implying a **Gaussian copula** with **equicorrelation**: any pair has correlation $\dfrac{\sigma_z^2}{\sigma_z^2 + \sigma_\varepsilon^2}$. For any other choice of $F_z, F_\varepsilon$ the joint distribution of $\mathbf{X}$, and more importantly its copula, has **no closed form**. Regardless of distributions, the simple structure makes every pair $(X_i, X_j)$ share the *same* bivariate copula — the copula is **equidependent** (a.k.a. **exchangeable** in copula terminology). One simulates from $F_z, F_\varepsilon$ to extract copula properties (rank correlation, Kendall's tau, quantile dependence) for use in SMM.
^def-gaussian

> [!definition] Single-factor with flexible weights (heterogeneous dependence)
> Allow the loading on the common factor to differ across variables:
> $$ X_i = \beta_i Z + \varepsilon_i, \quad i = 1, 2, \dots, N $$
> $$ Z \sim F_z, \quad \varepsilon_i \sim \text{iid } F_\varepsilon, \quad Z \perp\!\!\!\perp \varepsilon_i \;\forall i $$
> with $F_z, F_\varepsilon$ unchanged. The implied copula is **no longer equidependent**: pairs can have stronger or weaker dependence than others. This introduces $N-1$ additional parameters (the $\beta_i$), trading flexibility for a harder estimation problem. An intermediate **block-equidependence** model assigns common loadings to ex-ante groups (e.g. by industry) — see [[Multi-Factor and Block Dependence Structures]].
^def-flexible

> [!definition] Non-linear factor copulas nest known copulas
> The linear additive form generalizes to $X_i = h(Z, \varepsilon_i)$ for $h:\mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}$. With judicious choices of $h$, $F_Z$, $F_\varepsilon$, this nests standard **closed-form** copulas (McNeil et al. 2005, Ch. 5):
>
> | Copula | $h(Z,\varepsilon)$ | $F_Z$ | $F_\varepsilon$ |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Normal | $Z+\varepsilon$ | $N(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2)$ | $N(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2)$ |
> | Student's $t$ | $Z^{1/2}\varepsilon$ | $Ig(\nu/2,\nu/2)$ | $N(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2)$ |
> | Skew $t$ | $\lambda Z + Z^{1/2}\varepsilon$ | $Ig(\nu/2,\nu/2)$ | $N(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2)$ |
> | Gen. hyperbolic | $\gamma Z + Z^{1/2}\varepsilon$ | $GIG(\lambda,\chi,\psi)$ | $N(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2)$ |
> | Clayton | $(1+\varepsilon/Z)^{-\alpha}$ | $\Gamma(\alpha,1)$ | $Exp(1)$ |
> | Gumbel | $-(\log Z/\varepsilon)^\alpha$ | $Stable(1/\alpha,1,1,0)$ | $Exp(1)$ |
>
> where $Ig$ = inverse gamma, $GIG$ = generalized inverse Gaussian, $\Gamma$ = gamma. These have closed-form densities via specific combinations. **Removing the closed-form requirement** and using simulation-based estimation yields a much wider model class — the paper focuses on **linear additive** copulas with flexibly-specified factor distributions (especially skew $t$).
^def-nonlinear

## Examples

> [!example] Variance share of the common factor
> **Setup:** With $X_i = Z + \varepsilon_i$ and $\sigma_z^2 = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 = 1$, the common factor $Z$ accounts for one-half of the variance of each $X_i$.
> **Result:** In the Gaussian case this gives an equicorrelation of $\sigma_z^2/(\sigma_z^2+\sigma_\varepsilon^2) = 0.5$.
> **Interpretation:** $\sigma_z^2$ directly governs the *strength* of common dependence; the *shape* parameters of $F_z$ (degrees of freedom $\nu$, skew $\lambda$) govern the *tail and asymmetry* of dependence.
