# Copula Architecture Comparison

> [!summary]
> Five major copula architectures compete in moderate-to-high-dimensional econometric applications: **Gaussian**, **Student-$t$**, **Archimedean**, **factor copulas** (Oh & Patton 2012), and **vine copulas** (Aas et al. 2009). They differ in tail dependence, asymmetry, heterogeneous pairwise dependence, scalability, and estimation method. This note synthesises the key trade-offs so a practitioner can choose the right architecture for their application.

## Overview

No single copula architecture dominates in all dimensions. The choice depends on the data dimension $d$, the known or suspected dependence structure (factor-driven vs idiosyncratic), the importance of tail asymmetry, and computational budget. This note provides the comparison; the individual architectures are detailed in their own notes.

## Main Content

### Taxonomy of Copula Architectures

> [!definition] Five copula architecture families
>
> **1. Elliptical copulas (Gaussian, Student-$t$)**
> The copula is the dependence structure of a multivariate Gaussian or Student-$t$ distribution. Parameters: the $d\times d$ correlation matrix $\boldsymbol{P}$ (with $d(d-1)/2$ free entries) and, for $t$, a single degrees-of-freedom parameter $\nu$.
>
> **2. Archimedean copulas (Clayton, Gumbel, Frank, Joe)**
> The copula is generated by a single *generator function* $\phi$: $C(u_1,\ldots,u_d) = \phi^{-1}(\phi(u_1)+\ldots+\phi(u_d))$. The generator $\phi$ has 1-2 parameters. All pairwise dependences are *identical* (exchangeable).
>
> **3. Factor copulas (Oh & Patton 2012, [[Factor Copulas - Overview]])**
> Dependence generated by shared latent factor(s): $X_i = \beta_i Z + \varepsilon_i$. Parameters: the distribution of $Z$ (2-3 params) and $N$ loadings $\beta_i$ for the block model. Parsimony at high $N$.
>
> **4. C-Vine / D-Vine copulas (Aas et al. 2009, [[C-Vine and D-Vine Structures]])**
> Dependence decomposed into $d(d-1)/2$ bivariate pair copulas organized in star (C) or path (D) trees. Each pair copula independently specified. Sequential tree-by-tree MLE.
>
> **5. R-Vine copulas (Dissmann et al. 2013, [[R-Vine Structure Selection]])**
> Generalizes C- and D-vines: any spanning tree sequence satisfying the proximity condition. Structure selected data-adaptively by maximum spanning tree algorithm.
^def-taxonomy

### Systematic Comparison

> [!definition] Architecture comparison table
>
> | Criterion | Gaussian | Student-$t$ | Archimedean | Factor Copula | Vine (C/D/R) |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | **Tail dependence** | None ($\tau^U=\tau^L=0$) | Symmetric ($\tau^U=\tau^L>0$) | Asymmetric (Clayton: lower; Gumbel: upper) | Controllable via $F_Z$ | Per-pair: any family |
> | **Asymmetric tails** ($\tau^U \neq \tau^L$) | No | No | Partially (Clayton vs Gumbel) | Yes (skew-$t$ factor) | Yes (mix families) |
> | **Heterogeneous pairwise dep.** | Yes (different $\rho_{ij}$) | Yes (different $\rho_{ij}$) | No (exchangeable) | Partial (block loadings) | Full (each pair free) |
> | **Zero-tail-dep. pairs** | All | None | No | None | Yes (Gaussian pair copulas at any edge) |
> | **Estimation method** | MLE (closed form) | MLE (closed form) | MLE (closed form) | SMM (simulation-based) | Sequential MLE (h-function) |
> | **Parameters** | $O(d^2)$ | $O(d^2)+1$ | $O(1)$ | $O(d)$ to $O(K\cdot d)$ | $O(d^2)$ |
> | **Scalability** ($d \to \infty$) | Poor (corr. matrix proliferation) | Poor | Excellent (1-2 params) | Good | Moderate (structure selection) |
> | **Max practical $d$** | ~50 (regularized) | ~50 (regularized) | Any | ~500 | ~50 (full); ~200 (truncated) |
> | **Likelihood form** | Closed | Closed | Closed | None (simulate) | Tree-by-tree closed |
^def-comparison-table

### Tail Dependence: Key Qualitative Differences

> [!definition] Tail dependence comparison
>
> **Gaussian copula:** $\tau^U = \tau^L = 0$ for all $\rho < 1$. This is the fundamental weakness for financial data — assets can have strong linear correlation yet zero probability of *joint extreme events* under the Gaussian copula. This failure was prominent in 2007-08 CDO pricing (Li 2000 model).
>
> **Student-$t$ copula:** $\tau^U = \tau^L > 0$ for all $\nu < \infty$, decreasing toward zero as $\nu \to \infty$. Captures symmetric tail dependence (crashes as correlated as booms). Rejected for equities (Oh & Patton 2012: crashes more correlated than booms).
>
> **Factor copula with skew-$t$ factor:** $\tau^L \neq \tau^U$ — negative skew of $Z$ yields $\tau^L > \tau^U$ (crashes more correlated than booms). This is the key advantage over Student-$t$ copulas.
>
> **Vine copula:** each pair copula can independently be Clayton (lower tail), Gumbel (upper tail), $t$ (symmetric), or Gaussian (none). Maximum flexibility but $O(d^2)$ separate tail-dependence parameters to estimate.
^def-tail-comparison

### When to Use Each Architecture

> [!definition] Decision guide for copula architecture choice
>
> **Use Gaussian copula when:**
> - Linear dependence is a reasonable approximation
> - Tail events are not the focus of the analysis
> - $d$ is large ($d > 50$) and regularization is needed (shrinkage on $\boldsymbol{P}$, e.g. factor structure or graphical lasso)
> - Computational speed is critical (closed-form likelihood, fast MLE)
>
> **Use Student-$t$ copula when:**
> - Symmetric tail dependence is important (both crashes and booms correlated)
> - $d$ is moderate; no strong evidence of asymmetric dependence
> - Relatively few additional parameters vs Gaussian (just $\nu$)
>
> **Use Archimedean copula when:**
> - $d$ is very large and extreme parsimony is needed
> - Exchangeability (all pairs equally dependent) is acceptable
> - Only direction of tail dependence (lower vs upper) matters, not magnitude
>
> **Use factor copula when:**
> - There is a genuine *common-factor* interpretation (e.g. market index driving equities)
> - $d$ is large (50–500): $O(K \cdot d)$ parameters vs $O(d^2)$ for vines
> - Asymmetric or fat-tailed common factor is needed (skew-$t$ factor)
> - Pairwise heterogeneity can be captured by block structure
> - Closed-form likelihood is not available; SMM estimation is acceptable
>
> **Use vine copula when:**
> - $d$ is moderate (5–50) and pairwise dependences are genuinely *idiosyncratic*
> - Different pairs need different tail characters (e.g. commodities + equities + rates)
> - Conditional dependence structure (which pairs matter most once others are controlled) is of interest
> - Computational resources permit structure selection + sequential MLE
> - Interpretable pair-by-pair copula families are desired
^def-decision-guide

### Empirical Evidence from Oh & Patton (2012)

> [!example] S&P 100 copula model comparison (Oh & Patton 2012)
> **Data:** Daily returns of $N=100$ S&P 100 constituents, 2008–2010, $T=696$ observations.
>
> **Models compared:**
> - Gaussian copula (equidependence): rejected — $\hat{\tau}^U + \hat{\tau}^L$ significant
> - Student-$t$ copula (equidependence): partially rejected — $\hat{\tau}^U \neq \hat{\tau}^L$ (crashes > booms)
> - Skew-$t$ factor copula: fits well, captures asymmetric tail dependence
> - Block skew-$t$-$t$ factor copula (8 industry factors): best fit, AIC winner
> - Vine copula: mentioned as alternative; impractical at $N=100$ without strong structural assumptions (Oh & Patton note "hard-to-interpret/test assumptions" at this scale)
>
> **Key finding:** For $N=100$ equities driven by a market factor, the *factor copula* is the right architecture. Vine copulas are preferred when idiosyncratic heterogeneity is more important than common-factor structure.
