# Copula Architecture Comparison

> [!summary]
> High-dimensional copula models fall into four broad classes — **elliptical**, **Archimedean**, **factor**, and **vine** — each trading parametric parsimony against modelling flexibility in distinct ways. Elliptical and Archimedean copulas impose strong symmetry or exchangeability restrictions. Factor copulas (Oh & Patton 2017) are parsimonious and scale to 100+ dimensions via a latent common factor but force equidependence within factor groups. Vine copulas (Aas et al. 2009) are maximally flexible — each pair gets its own bivariate copula — but require $O(n^2)$ parameters that grow fast with dimension. The optimal architecture depends on dimension, interpretability requirements, and data volume.

## Overview

The Oh & Patton (2017) paper (see [[Factor Copulas - Overview]]) positions the factor copula explicitly against alternatives (its Sec. 2.3 "Position relative to the literature"). This note synthesises that comparison, extends it to classical copulas, and provides a decision framework. Understanding *why* each architecture was designed helps choose the right model for a given application.

## Main Content

> [!definition] Taxonomy of high-dimensional copula architectures
>
> | Architecture | Canonical model | Parameters | Tail dependence | Asymmetry | Exchangeability | Max tractable $n$ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | Gaussian copula | $C^{\text{Ga}}(\boldsymbol{\rho})$ | $n(n-1)/2$ | 0 | No | No | 100+ |
> | Student-$t$ copula | $C^{t}(\boldsymbol{\rho}, \nu)$ | $n(n-1)/2+1$ | Yes (equal $\lambda^U=\lambda^L$) | No | No | 100+ |
> | Grouped-$t$ copula | — | $n(n-1)/2+G$ | Yes (group-specific $\nu_g$) | No | No | 100+ |
> | Clayton copula | $C^{\text{Cl}}(\theta)$ | 1 | Lower tail only | Yes | Yes | Any |
> | Gumbel copula | $C^{\text{Gu}}(\theta)$ | 1 | Upper tail only | Yes | Yes | Any |
> | Frank copula | $C^{\text{Fr}}(\theta)$ | 1 | None | No | Yes | Any |
> | Factor copula (1-factor) | $X_i = Z + \varepsilon_i$ | $2 + N\text{(loadings)}$ | Yes (via heavy-tailed $F_z$) | Yes (via skew $F_z$) | Yes (simple) | 100+ |
> | Factor copula (block) | $K$-factor block | $2K + N\text{(loadings)}$ | Yes | Yes | Within-block | 100+ |
> | C-vine / D-vine | Pair-copula product | $n(n-1)/2 \times p$ | Per-pair | Per-pair | No | $\sim$20 |
> | R-vine | General vine | $n(n-1)/2 \times p$ | Per-pair | Per-pair | No | $\sim$50 (truncated) |
>
> *$p$ = parameters per bivariate family (1–2 typically).*
^def-taxonomy

> [!definition] Elliptical copulas: scalable but symmetric
> The Gaussian copula has zero tail dependence — no matter how strong the linear correlation, the probability of joint extreme events vanishes as marginal tail probabilities go to zero. The Student-$t$ copula adds symmetric tail dependence controlled by $\nu$: lower degrees of freedom produce stronger tail clustering. But both the upper and lower tail dependence coefficients are equal ($\lambda^U = \lambda^L$), implying crashes and booms are equally co-dependent — strongly rejected for equity returns (see [[Tail Dependence in Factor Copulas]]).
>
> The grouped-$t$ copula (Daul et al. 2003) allows different $\nu_g$ per group but still forces $\lambda^U = \lambda^L$ within groups.
^def-elliptical

> [!definition] Archimedean copulas: flexible tail but exchangeable
> An Archimedean copula is generated by a function $\phi: [0,1] \to [0,\infty)$ as $C(u_1,\ldots,u_n) = \phi^{-1}(\phi(u_1)+\cdots+\phi(u_n))$. This one-parameter construction forces **exchangeability**: all pairs share the same bivariate copula, which is rarely realistic for $n > 2$. Clayton captures lower tail clustering (co-crashes); Gumbel captures upper tail clustering; Frank has lighter tails and is symmetric. None of these admit asymmetric dependence ($\lambda^U \neq \lambda^L$) in the standard form.
^def-archimedean

> [!definition] Factor copulas: parsimonious common factor structure
> (Full treatment in [[Factor Copulas - Overview]], [[Factor Copula Construction]], [[Tail Dependence in Factor Copulas]])
>
> A $K$-factor copula generates the joint distribution via $X_i = \sum_{k=1}^K \beta_{ik} Z_k + \varepsilon_i$ and uses only the copula of $\mathbf{X}$. Key trade-offs:
> - **Parsimony:** $2K + N$ parameters for $K$ factors and $N$ variables. The equidependence (all $\beta_{ik} = 1$) model has just 2 parameters.
> - **Asymmetry:** A skewed factor $F_z$ produces $\lambda^U \neq \lambda^L$ analytically.
> - **No closed-form density:** Estimation requires SMM (see [[SMM Estimation of Factor Copulas]]).
> - **Equidependence within blocks:** All pairs within a factor group share the same bivariate copula shape — a restriction vine copulas do not impose.
^def-factor

> [!definition] Vine copulas: maximally flexible but parameter-hungry
> (Full treatment in [[Vine Copulas - Overview]], [[Vine Copula Construction and Density Factorization]])
>
> Vine copulas place *no* constraint on bivariate dependence structure between any pair of variables. Each of the $n(n-1)/2$ pair-copulas has its own family and parameter(s). Key trade-offs:
> - **Flexibility:** Each pair can have different tail dependence, asymmetry, and dependence strength.
> - **Curse of dimensionality:** $n(n-1)/2$ pair-copulas; for $n=100$ that is 4,950 bivariate models. Truncation at level $m$ reduces this to $\sum_{j=1}^{m}(n-j)$ pair-copulas; typical practice is $m \leq 5$.
> - **Closed-form density:** Given a structure and families, the joint likelihood evaluates analytically (via h-functions), enabling standard MLE.
> - **Structure ambiguity:** The "right" vine structure is unknown; greedy spanning-tree selection (Dissmann et al. 2013) is fast but not guaranteed optimal.
> - **Interpretability:** For large $n$, vine structures are hard to summarise — unlike a factor model where factor loadings have clear economic meaning.
^def-vine

> [!definition] When to use each architecture
>
> | Situation | Recommended architecture | Reason |
> |---|---|---|
> | $n > 50$, equidependence plausible | Factor copula (1-factor or block) | Scalable; $O(K)$ params; EVT tail results |
> | $n > 50$, heterogeneous dependence known | Block factor copula | Industry/geographic blocks with own factor |
> | $n \leq 20$, full flexibility needed | R-vine or D-vine | Each pair gets its own copula family |
> | One variable drives all others | C-vine | Root node mirrors factor structure |
> | Time-series, lag structure | D-vine | Path structure mirrors temporal order |
> | Symmetric dependence, fast estimation | Gaussian/Student-$t$ copula | Closed-form; elliptical; scalable |
> | Single-parameter reference model | Archimedean | Exchangeable; closed-form; testable |
> | Default high-dim financial | Block factor copula (skew-$t$ factor, $t$ idio.) | Asymmetric crashes, scalable, SMM |
^def-when-to-use
