# Copula Architecture Comparison

> [!summary]
> Five major copula architectures for $n \ge 3$ variables differ sharply in flexibility, parsimony, and computational cost. The **Normal and $t$ copulas** are simple but impose equidependence or at best block equidependence. **Archimedean copulas** (Clayton, Gumbel) have a single dependence parameter across all pairs — too restrictive for most real problems. **Factor copulas** (Oh & Patton 2012) use $O(NK)$ parameters and a latent-factor interpretation, but impose conditional independence given factors. **Vine copulas** (Aas et al. 2009) use $O(N^2/2)$ parameters and can mix different bivariate families at each pair — maximally flexible but computationally demanding in high dimensions.

## Overview

Sklar's theorem separates marginals from dependence, but says nothing about which copula class to choose. In moderate to high dimensions ($n \ge 5$) this choice is significant: it determines what dependence patterns can be captured, how many parameters must be estimated, and how fast estimation and simulation are.

Oh & Patton (2012) compare the four main alternatives to their factor-copula class in [[Factor Copulas - Overview#^literature|their literature review]], noting that vines are "hard-to-interpret/test assumptions" for very high dimensions ($n = 100$). This note maps out when each architecture is preferable.

## Main Content

### Copula architectures at a glance

> [!definition] Elliptical copulas (Normal, $t$)
> The **Gaussian copula** $C^{\text{Ga}}_\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ is the copula of a multivariate normal with correlation matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$. The **$t$-copula** $C^t_{\boldsymbol{\Sigma},\nu}$ is the copula of a multivariate $t$-distribution with $\nu$ degrees of freedom.
>
> - Parameters: $n(n-1)/2$ correlation parameters (plus $\nu$ for $t$). Grows as $O(n^2)$.
> - Tail dependence: **None** for Normal; **symmetric** $\tau^U = \tau^L > 0$ for $t$ (depends only on $\rho$ and $\nu$, same for every pair).
> - Restriction: All pairs share the same $\tau^U = \tau^L$ (for $t$). Cannot model asymmetric crashes.
> - Fit: Fast MLE on $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ via pseudo-observations (sample rank correlation).
> - Scale: $O(n^2)$ parameters limits practical use to $n \lesssim 30$ unless further restricted (e.g., factor structure on $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$).
^def-elliptical

> [!definition] Archimedean copulas (Clayton, Gumbel, Frank)
> An **Archimedean copula** is generated by a generator $\phi$:
> $$C(u_1,\ldots,u_n) = \phi^{[-1]}(\phi(u_1)+\cdots+\phi(u_n))$$
>
> - Parameters: **1 scalar** $\theta$ governs all pairwise dependence — equidependence forced.
> - Tail dependence: Lower only (Clayton), upper only (Gumbel), none (Frank).
> - Restriction: All pairs have identical dependence $\tau_{ij} = \tau$ for all $(i,j)$. Completely inappropriate for heterogeneous financial assets.
> - Scale: $O(1)$ parameters — trivially scalable to any $n$, but at the cost of severe inflexibility.
> - Use case: Low-dimensional problems (n ≤ 4) or when equidependence is a defensible assumption.
^def-archimedean

> [!definition] Factor copulas (Oh & Patton 2012)
> See [[Factor Copulas - Overview]] and [[Factor Copula Construction]] for full details. Summary for comparison:
>
> - Parameters: $K$ factor distributions + $N$ idiosyncratic distributions + $NK$ loadings = $O(NK)$ total. For $K = 1$: $O(N)$, a major parsimony gain over the vine.
> - Tail dependence: **Non-zero** iff common factor is heavy-tailed (e.g., $t$); **asymmetric** iff factor is skewed. Analytical EVT formulas (Propositions 1–3 in [[Tail Dependence in Factor Copulas]]).
> - Structural assumption: **Conditional independence** given factors — $X_i = \sum_k \beta_{ik} Z_k + \varepsilon_i$ with $\varepsilon_i \perp Z_k, \varepsilon_i \perp \varepsilon_j$. This is untestable directly but can be assessed via $J$-test on moment over-identification.
> - Estimation: Rank-based **SMM** (see [[SMM Estimation of Factor Copulas]]); no closed-form likelihood required.
> - Scale: Best-in-class for $n \gg 20$ (applied up to $n = 100$ by Oh & Patton 2012).
^def-factor

> [!definition] Vine copulas (Aas et al. 2009; Dißmann et al. 2013)
> See [[Vine Copulas - Overview]], [[C-vine and D-vine Structures]], [[Vine Copula Estimation and Selection]] for full details. Summary:
>
> - Parameters: $n(n-1)/2$ bivariate copulas, each possibly from a different family. With one-parameter families: $n(n-1)/2$ parameters, same as the elliptical copula. With truncation at level $p$: $O(pn)$ parameters.
> - Tail dependence: **Fully flexible** — depends on the bivariate families chosen. A $t$-copula edge gives symmetric tail dependence; Clayton gives lower; Gumbel gives upper; Normal gives none. *Different pairs can have different tail behaviour.*
> - Structural assumption: **Proximity condition** (valid R-vine tree structure) + **simplifying assumption** (conditional copulas do not depend on conditioning values). The simplifying assumption is routinely invoked but not always satisfied.
> - Estimation: Sequential or joint MLE using h-functions.
> - Scale: Practical for $n \lesssim 20$ without truncation; $n \lesssim 100$ with truncation ($p = 2$ or $3$).
^def-vine

### Decision guide

> [!example] Architecture selection flowchart
>
> **1. Is $n$ very large ($n > 50$)?**
> - Yes → **Factor copula** (Oh & Patton 2012). Only $O(NK)$ parameters; fastest estimation; admits analytical tail-dependence; tested up to $n = 100$. Vine copulas become computationally prohibitive.
>
> **2. Do you expect one dominant common factor (e.g., a market-wide crash risk)?**
> - Yes → **1-factor copula** with fat-tailed common factor ($t$ or skew-$t$). Parsimonious, interpretable, and captures the main economic intuition.
>
> **3. Do different asset pairs need fundamentally different copula families (e.g., sector A has upper-tail dependence, sector B has lower-tail dependence)?**
> - Yes → **Vine copula** (C-vine if one dominant variable; D-vine if sequential; R-vine via Dißmann for general). Each edge independently selects its family.
>
> **4. Is $n$ small ($n \le 5$) and dependence is well-approximated as equidependence?**
> - Yes → **Archimedean** (Clayton for lower-tail risk, Gumbel for upper, Frank for symmetric moderate). Simplest to estimate and simulate.
>
> **5. Do you need only symmetric tail dependence and have no strong asymmetry evidence?**
> - Yes → **$t$-copula** (optionally with a factor structure on $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ to reduce parameters). Simple, well-understood, fast.

### Quantitative comparison

| Property | Normal | $t$ | Archimedean | Factor ($K=1$) | Vine (no trunc.) |
|-----------|--------|-----|-------------|----------------|-----------------|
| Parameters ($n$ vars) | $n(n-1)/2$ | $n(n-1)/2 + 1$ | 1 | $O(n)$ | $n(n-1)/2$ |
| Tail dependence | None | Sym. (all equal) | Family-specific (all equal) | Fat factor → non-zero | Per-pair, any family |
| Asymmetric tail dep. | No | No | Clayton (lower); Gumbel (upper) | Skewed factor | Per-pair (via rotation) |
| Heterogeneous $\tau_{ij}$ | Yes | Yes | No | Partial (block) | Yes |
| Max practical $n$ | ~30 | ~30 | Any | ~100+ | ~20 (or ~100 truncated) |
| Estimation | MLE (fast) | MLE (fast) | MLE (trivial) | SMM (rank-based) | Sequential/joint MLE |
| Closed-form density | Yes | Yes | Yes | No | Yes (given structure) |
| Structural assumption | None | None | Equidep. | Cond. indep. | Simplifying assumption |

### Historical note and connections in the vault

The **Normal copula** was used in CDO pricing (Li 2000) and shown to catastrophically underestimate joint crash risk in 2007-2008. The factor copula (Oh & Patton 2012) responds by adding fat-tailed factors without losing parsimony. The vine copula (Aas et al. 2009) responds by allowing full flexibility at the pair level. Both directions are valid responses to the Normal copula's failure; the choice depends on dimension and the need for interpretability vs. flexibility.

The factor copula approach is thoroughly documented in the vault (six notes in [[../Dependence Modeling/_Index|Dependence Modeling]]). The vine copula approach is now covered by [[Vine Copulas - Overview]], [[C-vine and D-vine Structures]], and [[Vine Copula Estimation and Selection]].
