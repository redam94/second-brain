# Copula Architecture Comparison

> [!summary]
> Five major copula architectures serve high-dimensional applications: the **Gaussian** and **Student-$t$** (elliptical, closed-form, $O(d^2)$ parameters), **Archimedean** copulas (1 parameter, fully exchangeable, impractical above $d \approx 5$), **vine copulas** (pair-copula constructions, $d(d-1)/2$ bivariate building blocks, closed-form density, flexible), and **factor copulas** (latent factor structure, parsimonious, no closed-form density, scalable to $d \geq 100$). Choosing between them depends on dimensionality, the need for asymmetric/heterogeneous dependence, estimation strategy (MLE vs SMM), and interpretability.

## Overview

All copula models start from Sklar's theorem: separate the marginal distributions from the dependence structure. The architectures differ in how they parameterise the dependence structure $C(u_1, \ldots, u_d)$ and what properties (tail dependence, asymmetry, heterogeneity, parsimony) they impose or allow. No single architecture dominates; the right choice depends on $d$, data structure, and the research question.

### Architecture Overview Table

>
> | Architecture | Parameters | Tail Dependence | Asymmetry | Heterogeneous Pairs | Density | Estimation | Scalability |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | **Gaussian** | $d(d-1)/2$ correlations | None | No | Yes (per pair) | Closed form | MLE | Good to $d \approx 100$ |

### Gaussian and Student's $t$ Copulas

> [!definition] Elliptical copulas
> The **Gaussian copula** is the copula of the multivariate Normal distribution $N(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{R})$: $C^{Ga}(\mathbf{u}; \mathbf{R}) = \Phi_d(\Phi^{-1}(u_1), \ldots, \Phi^{-1}(u_d); \mathbf{R})$ where $\Phi_d$ is the multivariate Normal CDF. Properties:
> - Zero tail dependence for all pairs (joint extreme events are not more likely than product of marginals).
> - Symmetric: $C(u,v) = C(v,u)$ trivially; no asymmetry between booms and crashes.
> - $O(d^2)$ parameters in $\mathbf{R}$ — well-identified for large $T$.
>
> The **Student's $t$ copula** is the copula of the multivariate $t$ distribution: adds a single degrees-of-freedom parameter $\nu$. Properties:
> - Non-zero tail dependence: $\tau^U = \tau^L = 2 - 2t_{\nu+1}\!\left(\sqrt{(\nu+1)(1-\rho)/(1+\rho)}\right)$ — the same for *all* pairs.
> - Upper and lower tail dependence are always equal (symmetric).
> - $O(d^2)+1$ parameters.
>
> **When to use:** Gaussian copula for large $d$ when computation is paramount and tail dependence is not the focus. Student-$t$ when symmetric tail dependence is sufficient (often insufficient for equity returns where crashes are more correlated than booms).
^def-elliptical

### Archimedean Copulas

> [!definition] Archimedean copulas
> An Archimedean copula is generated by a **generator function** $\phi: [0,1] \to [0,\infty]$ (convex, decreasing, with $\phi(1) = 0$): $C(u_1,\ldots,u_d) = \phi^{-1}(\phi(u_1) + \cdots + \phi(u_d))$.
>
> | Family | Generator $\phi(t)$ | $\theta$ range | Tail dependence |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **Clayton** | $(t^{-\theta}-1)/\theta$ | $\theta > 0$ | Lower only: $\tau^L = 2^{-1/\theta}$, $\tau^U = 0$ |
> | **Gumbel** | $(-\log t)^\theta$ | $\theta \geq 1$ | Upper only: $\tau^U = 2 - 2^{1/\theta}$, $\tau^L = 0$ |
> | **Frank** | $-\log\frac{e^{-\theta t}-1}{e^{-\theta}-1}$ | $\theta \neq 0$ | None |
> | **Joe** | $-\log(1-(1-t)^\theta)$ | $\theta \geq 1$ | Upper only |
>
> Archimedean copulas are **fully exchangeable**: all pairs share the *same* bivariate copula. This is far too restrictive for most financial or economic applications with $d \geq 4$. Standard Archimedean copulas are practical only for $d \leq 4$; the "nested Archimedean" extension allows a hierarchy of generators but at the cost of much more complex construction.
>
> **When to use:** Small $d$ (bivariate or trivariate), when the single-generator assumption is acceptable, or as the pair-copula *family* within a vine (individual edges of a vine can use Clayton, Gumbel, etc.).
^def-archimedean

### Vine vs Factor Copulas: Head-to-Head

> [!definition] Vine copulas (pair-copula constructions)
> **Strengths:**
> - Maximum flexibility: each pair of variables can have its own bivariate copula from any family. Heterogeneous pairwise dependence is fully accommodated.
> - Closed-form density: the product-of-bivariate-densities formula enables standard MLE and AIC/BIC model selection.
> - Interpretable graph structure: the vine tree diagram shows which pairs drive dependence at each conditioning level.
> - Software maturity: `rvinecopulib` and `pyvinecopulib` are actively maintained, well-documented.
>
> **Weaknesses:**
> - $O(d^2)$ pair-copulas: for $d = 100$, there are 4950 bivariate copulas to fit — impractical without strong truncation.
> - Simplifying assumption: conditional copulas assumed not to depend on conditioning values; testable but often untested in practice.
> - Structure selection: finding the optimal R-vine structure is NP-hard in general; greedy heuristics (Dissmann) may miss the global optimum.
>
> **Practical limit:** $d \leq 20$–50 with truncation; $d \leq 10$–15 for full vines with family selection.
^def-vine-tradeoffs

> [!definition] Factor copulas (Oh & Patton 2017)
> **Strengths:**
> - Parsimonious: a $K$-factor model with block-equidependence uses $O(K \cdot d)$ parameters — feasible at $d = 100$.
> - Analytical tail dependence: EVT results (Propositions 1–3 in [[Tail Dependence in Factor Copulas]]) give closed-form $\tau^L, \tau^U$.
> - Asymmetry: skewed factor distributions capture crashes-more-correlated-than-booms.
> - Interpretable latent structure: the common factor $Z$ has an economic interpretation (market factor, credit factor).
>
> **Weaknesses:**
> - No closed-form density: estimation requires SMM with simulated moments — more infrastructure than vine MLE.
> - Equidependence within blocks: block factor copula imposes equal pairwise dependence within industry sectors; cannot capture pair-specific heterogeneity.
> - Requires explicit model choice for factor/idiosyncratic distributions ($F_z, F_\varepsilon$).
>
> **Practical sweet spot:** $d \geq 20$, especially $d \geq 50$; settings where a common-factor interpretation is natural (equity returns, credit risk, systemic risk).
^def-factor-tradeoffs

### Decision Guide

> [!definition] Architecture selection guide
>
> | Scenario | Recommended Architecture |
> |---|---|
> | $d \leq 5$, flexible tail/asymmetry per pair | **Vine copula** (D- or C-vine, any family) |
> | $5 < d \leq 20$, full bivariate flexibility needed | **Vine copula** (R-vine with truncation) |
> | $d > 20$ with one dominant common factor (e.g. market returns) | **Factor copula** (Oh & Patton skew $t$-$t$ block) |
> | $d > 20$ with several thematic groups | **Multi-factor block copula** (Oh & Patton, industry blocks) |
> | Fast computation, no tail dependence required | **Gaussian copula** |
> | Symmetric tail dependence needed, $d$ large | **Student's $t$ copula** |
> | $d \leq 5$, single-generator structure acceptable | **Archimedean copula** (Clayton, Gumbel, Frank) |
> | Time series, ordered variables | **D-vine copula** or **D-vine copula regression** |
^def-guide
