# Copula Architecture Comparison

> [!summary]
> This note synthesises the trade-offs across the major copula architectures for multivariate economic/financial data: **elliptical** (Gaussian, Student-$t$), **Archimedean** (Clayton, Gumbel, Frank), **factor copulas** (Oh & Patton 2017), and **vine copulas** (Aas et al. 2009). The central axes are: dimensionality ($d$), tail dependence flexibility, parameter parsimony, and estimation tractability. No architecture dominates all axes; the practical choice depends on $d$, data volume $T$, and the dependence features the application requires.

## Overview

High-dimensional dependence modeling lacks a universal copula: every architecture makes trade-offs. The figure below summarises the landscape:

```
Dimension (d):        2-5             5-20           20-100          100+
Copula architecture:  Any bivariate   Vine (R-vine)  Factor copula   Factor copula
                      family          VineCopula     (block, multi)  (equidep.)
```

The vault's existing Dependence Modeling notes cover factor copulas (Oh & Patton 2012/2017) comprehensively; vine copulas (Aas et al. 2009) are the newly added architecture. This note provides the comparison that practitioners need to choose between them.

## Main Content

### Head-to-head summary table

| Property | Elliptical ($t$) | Archimedean | Vine (R-vine) | Factor (Oh & Patton) |
|----------|-----------------|-------------|---------------|----------------------|

| **Parameters** | $d(d+1)/2$ for $\Sigma$; 1 DoF | 1–2 | $d(d-1)/2$ pair copulas | $d$ loadings + 2–4 factor params |
| **Tail dependence** | Symmetric (same $\tau^U = \tau^L$) | Archimedean-specific | Any (per-pair family choice) | EVT-derived; asymmetric with skew factor |
| **Upper $\neq$ lower tail** | No | Clayton: lower only; Gumbel: upper only | Yes (mix families) | Yes (skew factor) |
| **Pairwise heterogeneity** | Yes (via $\Sigma_{ij}$) | No (exchangeable) | Yes (different families per pair) | Limited (block structure) |
| **Likelihood available** | Yes (closed form) | Yes | Yes (h-function recursion) | **No** (SMM required) |
| **Estimation** | MLE | MLE | Sequential MLE (Aas 2009) | SMM / rank-based moments |
| **Structure selection** | None (parameterised by $\Sigma$) | None | Dissmann algorithm | None |
| **Simulation** | Easy ($\Sigma$ Cholesky) | Clayton/Gumbel: frailty trick | Tree-by-tree via $h^{-1}$ | Easy (draw $Z, \varepsilon_i$) |
| **Tail dependence theory** | Analytic ($t$) | Analytic | Pair-by-pair | EVT (Props. 1-3 in [[Tail Dependence in Factor Copulas]]) |
| **Key references** | McNeil et al. (2005) | Nelsen (2006) | Aas et al. (2009) | Oh & Patton (2017) |

### Elliptical copulas: closed form but symmetric

The **Gaussian copula** has zero tail dependence: $\tau^U = \tau^L = 0$ for all $\rho < 1$. This means joint extremes are asymptotically independent — empirically rejected for most financial return pairs. The **Student-$t$ copula** has symmetric tail dependence $\tau^U = \tau^L = 2\bar{F}_\nu(t_{\nu+1}(\sqrt{(\nu+1)(1-\rho)/(1+\rho)}))$, where $\bar{F}_\nu$ is the survival $t$ CDF; it improves substantially on Gaussian but still forces $\tau^U = \tau^L$ for all pairs.

For the multivariate $t$ copula, the full correlation matrix $\Sigma$ requires $d(d-1)/2$ parameters — infeasible for $d > 20$ without further structure (grouped-$t$, factor structure on $\Sigma$).

### Archimedean copulas: exchangeability constraint

Archimedean copulas (Clayton, Gumbel, Frank, Joe) are generated by a single generator function $\phi$ and have $C(u_1,\ldots,u_d) = \phi^{[-1]}(\phi(u_1)+\ldots+\phi(u_d))$. They are **exchangeable** — every pair has the same bivariate copula — and have 1 or 2 parameters regardless of $d$. This parsimony is also a rigid constraint: they cannot model heterogeneous pairwise dependence. In financial applications, assets in different sectors typically have very different pairwise correlations, making Archimedean copulas appropriate only as single-factor building blocks (e.g., inside a vine or factor model).

> [!definition] Tail dependence of Archimedean copulas
>
> | Family | $\tau^L$ (lower) | $\tau^U$ (upper) |
> |--------|----------------|----------------|
> | Clayton($\kappa$) | $2^{-1/\kappa}$ | 0 |
> | Gumbel($\delta$) | 0 | $2 - 2^{1/\delta}$ |
> | Frank($\alpha$) | 0 | 0 |
> | Joe($\delta$) | 0 | $2 - 2^{1/\delta}$ |
>
> Clayton captures lower tail ("crash") dependence only; Gumbel captures upper tail ("boom") only; Frank has zero tail dependence. Mixing these families as pair copulas in a vine yields richer tail structures.
^def-archimedean-tail

### Vine copulas: flexibility vs. dimensionality

Vine copulas are the most flexible architecture: any combination of bivariate families can appear, and pairwise dependencies can be heterogeneous. Key trade-offs:

> [!concept] When to prefer vine copulas
> - **$d \leq 20$** with moderate sample size $T \geq 5d$
> - Heterogeneous pairwise dependence (different pairs have different tail structures)
> - A natural ordering of variables exists (time series → D-vine; market factor → C-vine)
> - Full likelihood-based inference desired (vine has a tractable density)
> - Interest in conditional independence structure (zero pair copulas in later trees)
^concept-vine-prefer

**Scalability:** For $d = 10$, a vine has 45 bivariate copulas; for $d = 20$, 190. Sequential estimation remains feasible, but structure selection (Dissmann) becomes computationally intensive for $d > 30$. Truncated vines (2- or 3-truncation) remain practical for $d \leq 50$.

### Factor copulas: parsimony at high dimension

> [!concept] When to prefer factor copulas
> - **$d \geq 30$**, especially $d \geq 50$
> - Low data volume $T/d$ ratio (vine's $O(d^2)$ pair copulas need $T \gg d^2$)
> - Equidependence or block-dependence structure is plausible
> - Tail dependence must be analytically characterised (EVT Propositions 1–3)
> - Simulation-based inference acceptable (no closed-form likelihood)
^concept-factor-prefer

Factor copulas are parsimonious: a single-factor equidependence model has 2–4 parameters regardless of $d$. A $K$-factor block model (as in [[Multi-Factor and Block Dependence Structures]] for S&P 100) has $K + d + \text{block params}$ ≈ $O(d)$ parameters — far fewer than $O(d^2)$ for vines. The price is a restrictive dependence structure: all pairs within a block have the same bivariate copula.

### Comparison on tail dependence

> [!example] Asymmetric tail dependence: skew factor vs. vine
> **Factor copula (Oh & Patton):** Asymmetric tail dependence arises from a **skewed common factor** $F_z = \text{skew-}t(\nu, \lambda)$ with $\lambda < 0$ (negative skew). Under this specification:
> - $\tau^L > \tau^U$ (crash dependence exceeds boom dependence)
> - All pairs share the same asymmetry ratio — controlled by $\lambda$ alone
> - Analytically characterized: Proposition 2 of [[Tail Dependence in Factor Copulas]]
>
> **Vine copula:** Asymmetric tail dependence arises from the **first-tree pair copula families**:
> - Pair $(i,j)$ in Tree 1 gets family Clayton (lower tail only) → $\tau^L_{ij} > 0$, $\tau^U_{ij} = 0$
> - Another pair gets family Gumbel (upper tail only) → $\tau^L_{ij} = 0$, $\tau^U_{ij} > 0$
> - Each pair independently asymmetric — maximum flexibility
> - No single analytical formula; must be simulated

### Mixed architectures

**Factor copula + vine for marginals:** The marginal filtering step in [[Factor Copula Application - S&P 100 and Systemic Risk]] uses AR(1)-GJR-GARCH marginals. One can also use a vine copula for a subset of strongly-dependent stocks and a factor copula for the remainder.

**Nested Archimedean copulas:** Hierarchical Archimedean copulas (HAC) nest Archimedean copulas in a tree structure — a precursor to vine copulas but with less flexibility (all Archimedean building blocks must come from the same family for the model to remain a valid copula).

### Practical decision guide

```
START: How large is d?
│
├─ d ≤ 5: Use any bivariate family (bivariate extension) or 3-5 parameter vine
│
├─ 5 < d ≤ 20:
│   ├─ Heterogeneous pairs? → R-vine (Dissmann + AIC selection)
│   ├─ Natural ordering? → D-vine (time series, spatial)
│   ├─ One dominant variable? → C-vine
│   └─ Simple structure OK? → Multivariate t or Archimedean
│
├─ 20 < d ≤ 50:
│   ├─ Can tolerate SMM? → Factor copula (block model)
│   └─ Need likelihood? → Truncated vine (k=2 or 3)
│
└─ d > 50:
    → Factor copula (equidependence or block)
    → Gaussian copula (if tail dependence not critical)
```
