Abstract (from published version):
  Building on the work of Bedford, Cooke and Joe, we show how multivariate
  data, which exhibit complex patterns of dependence in the tails, can be
  modeled using a cascade of pair-copulae, acting on two variables at a time.
  We use the pair-copula decomposition of a general multivariate distribution
  and propose a method to perform inference. The models we consider may be
  represented as a structured collection of trees (regular vine). The approach
  is flexible in that it allows any bivariate copula to be used as the building
  block for the pair-copula decomposition, enabling tail dependence and
  asymmetry to be modelled simultaneously and at different strength for
  different pairs of variables, thus overcoming the limitation of the
  multivariate Gaussian or Student-t copulas.

  We present a sequential estimation method, which proceeds tree by tree,
  and is easily applied to large data sets. Pairs in higher trees are modelled
  using conditional copulae based on the 'simplifying assumption'. The method
  is illustrated on financial return data.

Key contributions:
  1. Practical statistical framework for pair-copula constructions (PCCs)
  2. Sequential (tree-by-tree) estimation algorithm
  3. h-function recursions for computing conditional CDFs
  4. Joint MLE as alternative to sequential estimation
  5. Application to financial return data (4 stock indices)
