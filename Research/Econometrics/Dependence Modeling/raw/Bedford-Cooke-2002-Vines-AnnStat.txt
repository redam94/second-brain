Abstract (paraphrased from published version):
  Bedford & Cooke introduce a new graphical model for multivariate distributions called
  a vine, which represents the joint density as a product of bivariate copulas. They
  show that regular vines (R-vines) provide a flexible framework for organising nested
  trees of pair copulas, and that any joint density satisfying regularity conditions can
  be represented as a vine. They characterise two important special cases — canonical
  vines (C-vines) and drawable vines (D-vines) — and establish the proximity condition
  required for regular vines. The paper also develops a vine-based sampling algorithm.

Key contributions:
  1. Introduces the vine graph as a sequence of trees T_1, T_2, ..., T_{n-1}.
  2. Establishes the proximity condition for regular vines (R-vines).
  3. Proves that any regular vine gives a valid joint density factorisation.
  4. Characterises C-vines (star trees) and D-vines (path trees) as special cases.
  5. Develops the vine-based sampling algorithm via conditional CDF transforms.
