# Bedford & Cooke (2002) — Vines: A New Graphical Model for Dependent Random Variables

**Full citation:** Bedford, T., & Cooke, R. M. (2002). Vines: A new graphical model for dependent random variables. *Annals of Statistics*, 30(4), 1031–1068.

**Open access:** Project Euclid (Annals of Statistics back issues are open access after 3 years).
Free PDF: https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-statistics/volume-30/issue-4/Vines--a-new-graphical-model-for-dependent-random-variables/10.1214/aos/1031689016.pdf

**See also:** Bedford, T., & Cooke, R. M. (2001). Probability density decomposition for conditionally dependent random variables modeled by vines. *Annals of Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence*, 32(1), 245–268. (The 2001 companion paper introducing vine densities.)

## Abstract

Vines are a new graphical model for representing dependent random variables.
They generalize the simple undirected graphical models used in the statistics
and AI literature, allowing more flexible representations of dependent structure.
The paper defines regular vines, the proximity condition, and proves that any
regular vine uniquely determines a joint density (up to specification of pair
copulas). The key theoretical result is that all conditional independence
restrictions implied by a vine can be characterized graphically via the
proximity condition.

## Key contributions

1. Defined vines as sequences of nested trees $T_1, T_2, \ldots, T_{d-1}$ satisfying
   the proximity condition.
2. Proved that a regular vine on $d$ nodes induces a unique factorisation of the
   joint density into $d(d-1)/2$ pair copulas.
3. Distinguished three canonical vine families: C-vines, D-vines, and general
   regular vines (R-vines).
4. Established the link between vine structure and conditional independence
   (simplified vines).
