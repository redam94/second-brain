# Tail Dependence in Factor Copulas

> [!summary]
> Despite the lack of a closed-form copula, the **tail dependence** of a factor copula can be derived analytically using extreme value theory (EVT), exploiting the simple linear factor structure. When the common factor and idiosyncratic shocks have **regularly varying (fat) tails** with a common tail index, the factor copula generates non-zero tail dependence; **asymmetry** of the common factor (skew) makes upper and lower tail dependence differ — capturing crashes being more correlated than booms. Proposition 2 gives the explicit coefficients for the skew $t$-$t$ factor copula.

## Overview

**Tail dependence** measures the probability that two variables are jointly extreme. For $X_i, X_j$ with marginals $G_i, G_j$:
$$
\tau^L_{ij} \equiv \lim_{q\to 0} \frac{\Pr[X_i \le G_i^{-1}(q),\, X_j \le G_j^{-1}(q)]}{q}, \qquad \tau^U_{ij} \equiv \lim_{q\to 1} \frac{\Pr[X_i > G_i^{-1}(q),\, X_j > G_j^{-1}(q)]}{1-q}
$$
Lower (upper) tail dependence is the probability both variables lie below (above) their $q$-quantile as $q \to 0$ ($q \to 1$), scaled by the probability that one does. The Normal copula has $\tau^L = \tau^U = 0$. The key insight: under regularly varying tails, the probability of the *sum* $\beta Z + \varepsilon$ exceeding a diverging threshold equals the sum of the component exceedance probabilities (EVT), and joint exceedance of two sums is driven entirely by the *shared* component $Z$.

## Main Content

> [!definition] Regularly varying tails (tail index)
> A distribution $F$ has **regularly varying tails with tail index $\alpha > 0$** if its tail probabilities decay like a power law: $\Pr[\cdot > s] \approx A\, s^{-\alpha}$ as $s \to \infty$, where $A>0$ is a constant. Equivalently the density satisfies $f(s) \approx \alpha A s^{-\alpha-1}$, so $A = \lim_{s\to\infty} f(s)/(\alpha s^{-\alpha-1})$. Fat-tailed distributions (Student's $t$, skew $t$) are regularly varying; the tail index equals the degrees of freedom $\nu$. The Normal has thinner-than-power-law tails and is *not* regularly varying (giving zero tail dependence).
^def-regvar

> [!theorem] Proposition 1 — Tail dependence for a single-factor copula
> Consider the factor copula generated by $X_i = \beta_i Z + \varepsilon_i$. Suppose $F_z$ and $F_\varepsilon$ have **regularly varying tails with a common tail index $\alpha > 0$**:
> $$
> \Pr[Z>s] = A_z^U s^{-\alpha},\;\; \Pr[\varepsilon_i>s] = A_\varepsilon^U s^{-\alpha} \;\;\text{as } s\to\infty
> $$
> $$
> \Pr[Z<-s] = A_z^L s^{-\alpha},\;\; \Pr[\varepsilon_i<-s] = A_\varepsilon^L s^{-\alpha} \;\;\text{as } s\to\infty
> $$
> where $A_z^L, A_z^U, A_\varepsilon^L, A_\varepsilon^U > 0$ are positive constants. Then:
>
> **(a)** if $\beta_j \ge \beta_i > 0$ (same sign, positive):
> $$
> \tau^L_{ij} = \frac{\beta_i^\alpha A_z^L}{\beta_i^\alpha A_z^L + A_\varepsilon^L}, \qquad \tau^U_{ij} = \frac{\beta_i^\alpha A_z^U}{\beta_i^\alpha A_z^U + A_\varepsilon^U}
> $$
>
> **(b)** if $\beta_j \le \beta_i < 0$ (same sign, negative):
> $$
> \tau^L_{ij} = \frac{|\beta_i|^\alpha A_z^U}{|\beta_i|^\alpha A_z^U + A_\varepsilon^L}, \qquad \tau^U_{ij} = \frac{|\beta_i|^\alpha A_z^L}{|\beta_i|^\alpha A_z^L + A_\varepsilon^U}
> $$
>
> **(c)** if $\beta_i \beta_j = 0$, or **(d)** if $\beta_i \beta_j < 0$ (opposite signs), then both tail dependence coefficients are **zero**.
>
> *Interpretation:* when loadings share sign and factor/idiosyncratic tail indices match, the copula has both upper and lower tail dependence. If $Z$ or $\varepsilon$ is **asymmetric** (so $A^U \ne A^L$), then $\tau^U \ne \tau^L$ — the model captures differing probabilities of joint crashes vs joint booms. Note tail dependence depends only on the *smaller* loading $\min\{|\beta_i|,|\beta_j|\}$.
^prop1

> [!theorem] Boundary case (unequal tail indices)
> If the tail index of $Z$ is **strictly greater** than that of $\varepsilon$ and $\beta_i\beta_j > 0$, then tail dependence equals **one** (the common factor dominates the joint tail). If the tail index of $Z$ is **strictly less** than that of $\varepsilon$, then tail dependence is **zero** (idiosyncratic tails dominate).
^prop1-boundary

> [!theorem] Proposition 2 — Tail dependence for a skew $t$-$t$ factor copula
> Consider the factor copula with $F_z = \text{Skew } t(\nu, \lambda)$ (Hansen 1994) and $F_\varepsilon = t(\nu)$ (standardized Student's $t$). Then the tail indices of $Z$ and $\varepsilon_i$ both equal $\nu$ (so $\alpha = \nu$), and the constants from Proposition 1 are:
> $$
> A_z^L = \frac{bc}{\nu}\left(\frac{b^2}{(\nu-2)(1-\lambda)^2}\right)^{-(\nu+1)/2}, \qquad A_z^U = \frac{bc}{\nu}\left(\frac{b^2}{(\nu-2)(1+\lambda)^2}\right)^{-(\nu+1)/2}
> $$
> $$
> A_\varepsilon^L = A_\varepsilon^U = \frac{c}{\nu}\left(\frac{1}{\nu-2}\right)^{-(\nu+1)/2}
> $$
> where
> $$
> a = 4\lambda c\,\frac{\nu-2}{\nu-1}, \qquad b = \sqrt{1 + 3\lambda^2 - a^2}, \qquad c = \frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\nu+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{\nu}{2}\right)\sqrt{\pi(\nu-2)}}
> $$
> Substituting these constants into Proposition 1 gives the tail dependence coefficients for this copula. Because the idiosyncratic shock is symmetric ($A_\varepsilon^L = A_\varepsilon^U$) but the factor is skewed ($A_z^L \ne A_z^U$ when $\lambda \ne 0$), upper and lower tail dependence differ; with $\lambda < 0$, lower tail dependence exceeds upper. When $\lambda = 0$ the skew $t$ reduces to standardized $t$ ($a=0$, $b=1$), giving the symmetric $A_\varepsilon^U = A_\varepsilon^L$ form.
^prop2

> [!theorem] Proposition 3 — Tail dependence for a multi-factor copula
> Consider the $K$-factor copula $X_i = \sum_{k=1}^K \beta_{ik} Z_k + \varepsilon_i$. Assume $F_\varepsilon, F_{z_1}, \dots, F_{z_K}$ have regularly varying tails with common tail index $\alpha>0$ and upper/lower coefficients $A_\varepsilon^U, A_1^U,\dots,A_K^U$ and $A_\varepsilon^L, A_1^L,\dots,A_K^L$. Then if $\beta_{ik} \ge 0$ for all $i,k$:
> $$
> \tau^L_{ij} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K \mathbf{1}\{\beta_{ik}\beta_{jk}>0\}\, A_k^L \beta_{ik}^\alpha \delta_{L,ijk}^\alpha}{A_\varepsilon^L + \sum_{k=1}^K A_k^L \beta_{ik}^\alpha}, \qquad \tau^U_{ij} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^K \mathbf{1}\{\beta_{ik}\beta_{jk}>0\}\, A_k^U \beta_{ik}^\alpha \delta_{U,ijk}^\alpha}{A_\varepsilon^U + \sum_{k=1}^K A_k^U \beta_{ik}^\alpha}
> $$
> where the adjustment factors $\delta$ account for the (generally distinct) thresholds when loadings differ:
> $$
> \delta_{L,ijk}^{-1} \equiv \begin{cases} \max\{1, \gamma_{L,ij}\beta_{ik}/\beta_{jk}\}, & \text{if } \beta_{ik}\beta_{jk}>0 \\ 1, & \text{if } \beta_{ik}\beta_{jk}=0 \end{cases}, \qquad \delta_{U,ijk}^{-1} \equiv \begin{cases} \max\{1, \gamma_{U,ij}\beta_{ik}/\beta_{jk}\}, & \text{if } \beta_{ik}\beta_{jk}>0 \\ 1, & \text{if } \beta_{ik}\beta_{jk}=0 \end{cases}
> $$
> $$
> \gamma_{L,ij} \equiv \left(\frac{A_\varepsilon^L + \sum_{k=1}^K A_k^L \beta_{jk}^\alpha}{A_\varepsilon^L + \sum_{k=1}^K A_k^L \beta_{ik}^\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}, \qquad \gamma_{U,ij} \equiv \left(\frac{A_\varepsilon^U + \sum_{k=1}^K A_k^U \beta_{jk}^\alpha}{A_\varepsilon^U + \sum_{k=1}^K A_k^U \beta_{ik}^\alpha}\right)^{1/\alpha}
> $$
> Only factors loading on *both* variables ($\beta_{ik}\beta_{jk}>0$) contribute to joint tail dependence. Unlike the one-factor case, a general ranking of thresholds cannot be obtained, so the $\delta$ terms have no simple closed form (computed via the $\gamma$ expressions). This proposition is used to compute the implied inter- and intra-industry tail dependence in the empirical application.
^prop3

> [!definition] Proof sketch (Appendix A — EVT mechanics)
> The driving EVT fact (Hyung & de Vries 2007): for regularly varying tails, the exceedance probability of a sum equals the sum of component exceedance probabilities as the threshold diverges, $\Pr[\beta Z + \varepsilon > s] \approx s^{-\alpha}(A_z^U \beta^\alpha + A_\varepsilon^U)$, since $\Pr[\beta Z > s] \approx A_z^U(s/\beta)^{-\alpha}$. For the *joint* numerator, $\Pr[\beta Z + \varepsilon_1 > s,\, \beta Z + \varepsilon_2 > s] \approx \Pr[\beta Z > s,\, \beta Z > s] = s^{-\alpha} A_z^U \beta^\alpha$ — only the **shared** component $Z$ can drive both into the tail simultaneously (the independent $\varepsilon_i$ jointly-exceeding is higher order $o(s^{-\alpha})$). The ratio gives $\tau^U$. When loadings differ, thresholds $s_1, s_2$ are matched so $G_1(s_1)=G_2(s_2)$, diverging at the same rate; the proof tracks which of $s_i/\beta_i$ is larger. Proposition 2 differentiates the regularly-varying density of Hansen's (1994) skew $t$ to extract $A_z^U, A_z^L$ (verified via Mathematica).
^proof-sketch

## Examples

> [!example] Quantile dependence and the flexibility of skew $t$-$t$
> **Setup:** Quantile dependence $\tau_q$ (the empirical proxy that converges to tail dependence as $q\to 0$ or $q\to 1$):
> $$
> \tau_q \equiv \begin{cases} \frac{1}{q}\Pr[U_1\le q,\, U_2 \le q], & q\in(0,0.5] \\ \frac{1}{1-q}\Pr[U_1>q,\, U_2>q], & q\in(0.5,1) \end{cases}, \qquad U_i \equiv G_i(X_i)\sim \text{Unif}(0,1)
> $$
> **Result (Figure 2):** Symmetric copulas (Normal, $t$-$t$) have $\tau_q$ symmetric about $q=0.5$; fat-tailed factors raise $\tau_q$ near both tails. The skew $t(4)$-$t(4)$ factor copula generates **weak upper but strong lower** quantile dependence.
> **Interpretation:** Near a tail, an extreme observation in one variable is more likely to have come from the fat-tailed common factor $Z$ than from the thin-tailed idiosyncratic $\varepsilon_i$, making an extreme in the other variable more likely. Negative skew concentrates this in the lower tail — exactly the "crashes more correlated than booms" feature wanted for equity returns.
