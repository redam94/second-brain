# Gelman & Loken (2013) — Front Matter, Borges Epigraph and References

> [!summary]
> This note records the parts of the paper that are not argument: the title and its literary source, the Borges epigraph, the abstract, the author affiliations and acknowledgments (p. 1), and the full reference list of **31 entries** (pp. 15-17). For each reference it records where the paper cites it and which note in this set covers that passage. Three references are listed but never cited in the text: Gelman (2014), Gelman & Tuerlinckx (2000) and Gelman & Weakliem (2009).
^summary

## Overview

The paper is a 17-page working paper dated **14 Nov 2013**. Its full title is "The garden of forking paths: Why multiple comparisons can be a problem, even when there is no 'fishing expedition' or 'p-hacking' and the research hypothesis was posited ahead of time" (p. 1). The argument itself is summarised in [[Gelman Loken 2013 - Garden of Forking Paths - Overview]].

## Main Content

### Title and epigraph (p. 1)

> [!quote] Epigraph (p. 1)
> "I thought of a labyrinth of labyrinths, of one sinuous spreading labyrinth that would encompass the past and the future . . . I felt myself to be, for an unknown period of time, an abstract perceiver of the world." — Borges (1941)
^quote-gl-borges

**Where the title comes from.** The reference list (p. 15) gives the source as Borges, J. L. (1941), *El jardín de senderos que se bifurcan* (spelled "El jardin" without the accent in the source), Buenos Aires: Sur. The English translation is by D. A. Yates (1964), in *Labyrinths: Selected Stories & Other Writings*, pp. 19-29, New York: New Directions. The Spanish title translates as "The Garden of Forking Paths", which is the paper's title. The image fits the argument: every analysis the researcher *could* have run, given other data, is a path in the labyrinth, and the p-value has to account for all of them (§4.1, p. 11; see [[Gelman Loken 2013 - Way Forward - Preregistration, Replication and Analyzing All Comparisons]]). The same story also names McElreath's unrelated [[Garden of Forking Data]].

### Abstract (p. 1)

The abstract makes three claims. Researcher degrees of freedom can produce a multiple-comparisons problem "even in settings where researchers perform only a single analysis on their data". The problem arises because there can be many potential comparisons "when the details of data analysis are highly contingent on data, without the researcher having to perform any conscious procedure of fishing or examining multiple p-values". The examples are published papers whose analysis decisions were "theoretically-motivated based on previous literature" but whose details of data selection and analysis "were not pre-specified and, as a result, were contingent on data."

### The three uncited references

The paper lists these three works but never cites them in the text. The descriptions below come from their titles and from other vault notes, not from the paper.

> [!note] Gelman (2014), varying treatment effects and unreplicable research (*Journal of Management*)
> Its title links unreplicable research to **treatment effects that vary** across people and settings, from a Bayesian perspective. It is a natural companion to §2: when effects vary, a fork such as "main effect or interaction" picks out whichever subgroup pattern the noise favours.
^note-gelman-varying-treatment-effects-and

> [!note] Gelman & Tuerlinckx (2000), Type S error rates (*Computational Statistics*)
> The source of the **Type S (sign) error** idea: the probability that a confident claim gets the *sign* of an effect wrong. It underlies the paper's remark (§4.1, p. 11) that even the *direction* of the reported effects might not reappear on replication. See [[Type S and Type M Errors]] and [[Multiple Comparisons - Bayesian Perspective]].
^note-gelman-tuerlinckx-type-s-error

> [!note] Gelman & Weakliem (2009), "Of beauty, sex, and power" (*American Scientist*)
> The beauty-and-sex-ratio example of estimating **small effects with small samples**, where significant estimates are badly exaggerated. It is the same low-power setting that §1.2 (p. 3) and §4.4 (pp. 14-15) describe. The vault works through this example in [[There Is No Safe Haven]].
^note-gelman-weakliem-of-beauty-sex
