# Griffiths 2011 - Equivalence Classes and the Infinite Limit

> [!summary]
> Binary matrices are identified up to column permutation via the left-ordered form $\text{lof}(Z)$. The limit $K\to\infty$ of $P([Z])$ exists and equals $\frac{\alpha^{K_+}}{\prod_{h=1}^{2^N-1}K_h!}\exp(-\alpha H_N)\prod_{k=1}^{K_+}\frac{(N-m_k)!(m_k-1)!}{N!}$.

## Main Content

> [!definition] History and left-ordered form
> The **history** of feature $k$ at object $i$ is $(z_{1k},\dots,z_{(i-1)k})$; the full history is $(z_{1k},\dots,z_{Nk})$, identified with its decimal value $h\in\{0,\dots,2^N-1\}$ (first row most significant). $K_h$ = number of columns with history $h$; $K_0$ = number of empty columns; $K_+=\sum_{h\ge1}K_h$; $K=K_0+K_+$. $\text{lof}(Z)$ orders columns left to right by the magnitude of the binary number each column expresses (first row most significant), so columns with $z_{1k}=1$ are grouped at the left (the paper's text also describes this as ordering by history); two matrices are **lof-equivalent** if $\text{lof}(Y)=\text{lof}(Z)$. The class is $[Z]$.

^def-lof-equivalence

The class $[Z]$ is preserved by permuting rows or columns (with the same permutation applied across class members). Class matrices generated by assignment vectors correspond exactly to partitions, so lof-classes are to binary matrices what partitions are to assignment vectors. Cardinality: $|[Z]|=\dfrac{K!}{\prod_{h=0}^{2^N-1}K_h!}$ (fewer if columns repeat).

> [!theorem] Infinite limit (Eqs. 12-14)
> $$
> P([Z])=\frac{K!}{\prod_{h=0}^{2^N-1}K_h!}\prod_{k=1}^K\frac{\frac\alpha K\Gamma(m_k+\frac\alpha K)\Gamma(N-m_k+1)}{\Gamma(N+1+\frac\alpha K)}
> $$
> and as $K\to\infty$
> $$
> P([Z])\to\frac{\alpha^{K_+}}{\prod_{h=1}^{2^N-1}K_h!}\exp\{-\alpha H_N\}\prod_{k=1}^{K_+}\frac{(N-m_k)!\,(m_k-1)!}{N!},\qquad H_N=\sum_{j=1}^N\frac1j.
> $$

^thm-ibp-limit

Proof sketch (Eqs. 13-14, Appendix A): with $x=\alpha/K$, an empty column has probability $x\Gamma(x)\Gamma(N+1)/\Gamma(N+1+x)=N!/\prod_{j=1}^N(j+x)$; a used column differs from this by the factor $x\,(N-m_k)!\prod_{j=1}^{m_k-1}(j+x)/N!$. Hence $P(Z)=\big[N!/\prod_j(j+x)\big]^K\prod_{k\le K_+}x(N-m_k)!\prod_{j<m_k}(j+x)/N!$. As $K\to\infty$: $\big[\prod_{j=1}^N(1+\tfrac{\alpha}{jK})\big]^{-K}\to e^{-\alpha H_N}$; $\prod_{j<m_k}(j+x)\to(m_k-1)!$; and $K!/(K_0!K^{K_+})\to1$ cancels the $K^{-K_+}$ left in $x^{K_+}$ to give $\alpha^{K_+}$. The result is exchangeable: only the multiset of histories matters.

## Examples
> [!example] Left-ordering a 3x4 matrix
> Columns with histories $(1,0,1)=5$, $(0,1,0)=2$, $(1,1,1)=7$, $(1,0,1)=5$. Sorted descending by binary value: $7,5,5,2$. Here $K_7=1,K_5=2,K_2=1$, so $|[Z]|=4!/(1!\,2!\,1!)=12$ distinct column orderings.
