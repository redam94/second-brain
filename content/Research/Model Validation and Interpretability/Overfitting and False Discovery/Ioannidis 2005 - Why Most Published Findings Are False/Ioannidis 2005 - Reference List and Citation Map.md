# Ioannidis 2005 - Reference List and Citation Map

> [!summary]
> This note transcribes all 37 references of Ioannidis (2005), p. 0701. For each one it records **where in the essay the reference is cited and what claim it supports**. Use it to look up the bracketed numbers in the other notes of this set. The references fall into five groups: refutation evidence (1-8), the Bayesian/PPV precursors (9-11), design and reporting standards (14-15, 18-25, 35), conflicts of interest and expert opinion (26-28), and high-throughput discovery and multiplicity (4, 8, 17, 29-34, 36-37).

## Overview

The essay makes its argument with formulas and backs its empirical premises with citations. The premises are that refutation is common, that conflicts of interest are common, that selective outcome reporting happens even in RCTs, and that the Proteus phenomenon is common in genetics. Mapping each reference to its claim shows which steps rest on outside evidence and which on the model alone. Much of that evidence comes from the author's own earlier work (refs 1, 5, 7, 8, 19, 29, 30, 36). This matters when weighing the essay's empirical claims; see [[Ioannidis 2005 - Remedies, Limitations and Later Critiques]].

## Examples

> [!example] Reading the map
> The core mathematical sections (Testing by Several Teams, Box 1, the null-field section) cite **nothing**. They are the essay's own derivations. The empirical premises are concentrated in the corollaries. Of the 37 references, 8 (1, 5, 7, 8, 19, 29, 30, 36) are co-authored by Ioannidis. For a model-validation reader, the methodological precursors that matter most are Wacholder et al. [10] (false positive report probability), Lindley [32] (large-$n$ significance for trivial effects) and Hsueh et al. [37] (estimating the number of true nulls, i.e. estimating $R$).
